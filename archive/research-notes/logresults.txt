I read the bundle. Below is a deep explanation of what the results actually say, what they do **not** say, and where the PDF has inconsistencies/garbled tables you should fix before publishing.

## Bottom line first

The bundle has **one strong descriptive/event-timing result**, **one important measurement result**, and **several weak or null causal tests**:

1. **Strongest:** New Zealand’s **author = publisher** share breaks upward around **2007** and ends far above the UK/US, consistent with free national ISBNs + POD/Amazon-era tools. But this is **not clean causal proof**.
2. **Strong measurement point:** In paid-ISBN countries, the Amazon-era self-publishing boom sits mostly **outside national bibliographies**. National ISBN shares measure **use of the national agency/catalogue**, not total self-publishing.
3. **Null/weak:** France vs Québec does **not** show a sustained break after France’s 2019 ISBN fee. Pre-2019 both were free, so the paid-vs-free test only exists post-2019, and the fee dose is small/unverified.
4. **Does not hold cleanly:** The **combined** UK vs NZ comparison reverses sign because UK services (AuthorHouse/Xlibris) spike and then move their stated place to the US. The NZ-vs-UK AP advantage disappears once country trends are allowed.
5. **Not validation:** “Amazon vs Bowker” is only **two Bowker points**, not a series; it is a plausibility check, not a validation.

Also critical: **AP = “author = publisher,” not Amazon Publishing.** The PDF says this explicitly on page 4. Do not read `ap_share_all.png` as Amazon Publishing.

## 1. Definitions that govern every result

These definitions determine whether the numbers mean anything:

- **AP / author = publisher:** a personal author’s normalized name equals or is contained in the publisher string, including initials/surname variants, or the publisher says “the author / à compte d’auteur / auto-édition.” Corporate author = publisher is flagged separately as `corporate_self` and **not counted**.
- **Self-published / combined:** AP **OR** a service imprint from `config/selfpub_services.csv` (AuthorHouse, Xlibris, CreateSpace, “Independently published,” Lulu, BoD, Édilivre, etc.).
- **Denominator:** distinct valid **print ISBN-13s of catalogued books** in the national catalogue for that country/year. It is **not** “all ISBNs issued,” **not** new titles only, and **not** sales. It includes reprints, new editions, government publications, standards unless a sensitivity variant excludes them. Excludes ebooks/audio/music/maps/serials/microforms.
- **Country attribution:** NZ via 008 country = nz; UK via publication-country links, Ireland/imports dropped; France via 102/place; Québec via place-name match; US LoC via 008/15–17 ending in “u.” Amazon has no country field.
- **Year:** publication year from the record, not cataloguing year.
- **Windows:** UK/NZ 1995–2014 (BNB 2015+ incomplete); France/Québec 2010–2021; US LoC 1995–2011; NZ break tests to 2019.

So every “share” is **share of catalogued print ISBNs**, not share of books sold, authors, or ISBNs issued. That is the central limitation.

## 2. Background facts and design consequences

- **NZ ISBNs:** free throughout; no charging period found.
- **UK:** Nielsen charges throughout (current £93 single, £174 for 10, etc.); no clear price history collected; no clear upward trend.
- **France:** ISBNs free until **8 March 2019**, then application fees began. Extra services stayed free.
- **Québec:** free for eligible francophone Canadian publishers/self-publishers; start date not established.
- **Legal deposit:** BnF/NZ/UK/Québec say self-published works are covered, but compliance is not measured.

**Design consequence:** The “paid vs free” framing holds for **UK vs NZ** for the whole window. For **France vs Québec**, it only holds **from 2019**; 2010–2018 compares **two free regimes**. That is why France/Québec cannot test the fee hypothesis before 2019.

## 3. UK vs NZ results

### What the figure/table shows
`uk_nz_shares.png` is the **combined** share: AP OR service imprint.

- **NZ:** rises from ~3.2% in 1995 to ~7.7% in 2007, ~13.6% in 2013, and ~16.2% in 2016. The rise is mostly **author = publisher**, not service imprints. NZ is smooth and not service-driven.
- **UK:** starts ~0.7–0.9%, rises to ~4.1% in 2011, then falls to ~2.2% by 2014. That rise is **service-driven** (AuthorHouse/Xlibris/CreateSpace etc.), and the fall is largely a **place-of-publication effect**: AuthorHouse records move from Milton Keynes/UK-place to Bloomington, Indiana/US-place, so they fall out of the UK country filter even though the BNB still catalogues them. UK AP alone is flat: ~0.87% in 1995 → ~1.0% in 2013.

So the UK combined series is a **lower bound on UK authors using services** after 2011, and the UK AP series is the steadier one.

### Econometric results
**M1 DiD / logit with year FE, year-clustered SEs:**

- **NZ vs UK, AP, break 2007, no country trends:** OR **1.79** [1.54, 2.10] — NZ odds higher.
- **With country trends:** OR **1.29**, p = **0.27** — **not significant**. So the NZ-vs-UK AP advantage is **not robust to pre-existing country trends**.
- **NZ vs US LoC, AP, no trends:** OR **2.98** [2.32, 3.61]; with trends OR **2.12**, p = **0.002**. Stronger, but US LoC is a selected catalogue.
- **NZ vs UK, combined:** OR **0.66** [0.52, 0.84] — **NZ lower** on the combined measure, because the UK service boom lifts the UK numerator.

**M2 unknown-break tests:**

- NZ AP break **2007**: F = 22.8, bootstrap p = **0.001**.
- NZ combined break **2007**: F = 23.0, p = **0.001**.
- UK AP break **2001**, combined **2007**; US LoC AP **2003**, combined **2004**; France AP **2009** p = 0.11, combined **2014** p = 0.025; Québec **none** (p > 0.3).

But these p-values use an **iid residual bootstrap**, which ignores serial correlation, so they are likely **too small**. A break year coinciding with Amazon/POD is **suggestive, not causal**.

**M3 NZ–UK interrupted time series on the log-odds gap:**

- AP at B = 2007: slope change −0.015, p = 0.57 — no clean break.
- B = 2008: −0.037, p = 0.09.
- B = 2009: −0.073, p = 0.002 — but this is a different break year and the combined series shows the opposite sign.
- So **no clean break in the NZ–UK gap**; NZ’s jump is partly shared by the UK.

**Interpretation:** NZ’s AP share clearly rises and breaks around 2007; free ISBNs + POD fit that. But the UK comparison is weak, and once trends are allowed the AP NZ-vs-UK effect is not significant. The combined measure reverses. This is **consistent with** the free-ISBN story, not proof of it.

## 4. France vs Québec results

### What the figure/table shows
`fr_qc_shares.png` is the combined share.

- **France:** ~6.2% in 2010, rises to ~14.9% in 2015, dips to ~8.9% in 2017, rises to ~15.5% in 2020, ~14.0% in 2021. The dip/spikes are heavily driven by **Édilivre** (service imprint): its records fall from ~6,100 in 2015 to 875 in 2017, then rise to 4,272 in 2018. So **do not read 2014–2018 France levels as a real trend**; the author=publisher series is steadier.
- **Québec:** ~3.8% in 2010 → ~9.6% in 2021; no big service spike; smoother.

Pre-2019 both were free. France rose faster than Québec **even though both were free**, which undermines a simple fee story before 2019. After France began charging in March 2019, France’s combined share was roughly flat (13.4→14.0%) while Québec rose (7.1→9.6%) — direction the fee hypothesis predicts, but only three post-fee years, one noisy break (Édilivre), and a Québec 2021 jump.

### Econometric results
**M4 France vs Québec:**

- Unit: year-level log-odds gap \(d_t = \text{logit}(p_{FR,t}) - \text{logit}(p_{QC,t})\), 2010–2021.
- **AP pre-trend 2010–2018:** slope **+0.021/yr**, Newey-West SE 0.005, p_NW = **0.004**, permutation p = **0.083**. So there is small but nonzero convergence; permutation evidence is marginal.
- Residual SD around trend: **0.083** — yearly noise ~8% in odds.
- Year-to-year changes not correlated (r = −0.10): parallel in trend, not in shocks.
- **Combined share:** gap slope **0.006/yr**, p = **0.86** — noisy, dominated by Édilivre.
- **Post-2019 AP deviations from extrapolated pre-trend:** 2019 +0.054, 2020 +0.422, 2021 +0.054; mean **+0.177 = 2.1 pre-residual SDs**.
- Piecewise regression: level shift p = **0.08**; slope change p = **0.9996**.
- So: **no sustained break**; at most a weak level shift, driven by 2020 (COVID year), and not a slope change.

**Interpretation:** France vs Québec does **not** provide a clean test of the 2019 fee. Pre-2019 both were free; post-2019 is three noisy years; the fee was small/unverified. The AP series is parallel-ish before 2019; the combined series is dominated by one service publisher. So the result is **null with qualification**, not support for a strong fee effect.

`sensitivity.png` and C3 are important.

**UK/NZ:**
- Across AP variants, **NZ stays above UK** in every variant: pooled NZ ~**7.1–8.4%**, UK ~**0.8–1.9%**.

**Interpretation:** The NZ-above-UK AP gap is robust. The France-above-Québec combined gap is **not** a robust AP result; it depends on service imprints.

# ELI5: What this whole study found

## First, the basic setup

Imagine there are **two ways** to get a book registered so it can be sold in shops:

- **The official government way** — you pay money (like a license fee) and get an official number for your book. That number is called an **ISBN**.
- **The free/cheap way** — some countries give you that number for free. And Amazon gives you one for free too if you publish through them.

The researchers wanted to know: **when ISBNs are free, do more people self-publish?**

That's it. That's the whole question.

## What they actually measured

They looked at big library lists of books from five places — **UK, New Zealand, France, Québec, and the US** — and tried to spot which books were self-published.

How? Mostly by looking for clues like:
- The author's name is also listed as the publisher (like "Published by John Smith" when John Smith wrote it)
- The publisher is a known self-publishing company (like AuthorHouse or Lulu)

Then they counted: **out of all the books in the library list that year, what fraction looked self-published?**

## The big finding: New Zealand

**New Zealand gives out ISBNs for free. The UK charges for them.**

And look what happened:

- In **New Zealand**, the self-publishing share exploded — from about 3% in the 1990s to over 13% by 2013.
- In the **UK**, it stayed tiny — around 1%.

New Zealand's self-publishing rate per person ended up roughly **10 times higher** than the UK's.

And the timing? New Zealand's numbers suddenly jumped around **2007** — which is right when Amazon's print-on-demand tools got popular.

**So: free ISBNs + easy printing = way more self-publishing.** That fits the story.

## But here's the catch (important!)

The UK comparison is **messy**, because:

- A lot of the UK's "self-publishing" was actually big companies like AuthorHouse.
- Around 2011–2012, those companies **moved their address** from the UK to Indiana, USA.
- So their books suddenly stopped counting as "UK" books — even though UK authors were still using them.
- That makes the UK numbers look like they **dropped**, when really it was just a paperwork/address thing.

And when the researchers tried to be more careful with the statistics (controlling for the fact that the two countries were already trending differently before 2007), **the New Zealand vs UK difference got weaker** — not significant anymore.

So the New Zealand story is **suggestive, not proven**.

## France vs Québec: the test that didn't work

The idea was: **France started charging for ISBNs in 2019, Québec stayed free. So France's self-publishing should drop, and Québec's should keep rising.**

But there's a problem:

**Before 2019, France's ISBNs were ALSO free.**

So for most of the study period, they were comparing **two free countries**. That can't test whether fees matter.

After 2019? France's share stayed roughly flat while Québec's rose a bit. That's the **direction** the theory predicts — but:

- Only 3 years of data
- One big French publisher (Édilivre) kept messing up the numbers
- 2020 was COVID, which messed everything up
- The fee was small

**Verdict: we can't tell if the fee did anything.** The result is basically "no clear answer."

## The Amazon finding (this one is solid)

Here's something important and a little surprising:

**In countries that charge for ISBNs (like the US and UK), the giant Amazon self-publishing boom is mostly INVISIBLE in the official library lists.**

Why? Because when you self-publish on Amazon, Amazon gives you a **free ISBN**. You don't need the official one. So the official library lists only see a tiny fraction of what's actually being published.

The numbers are wild:
- Amazon shows about **24,000** self-published books per year in the UK
- The UK's official library list shows only about **35–57**

So: **the official lists are measuring "who bothered to use the official system," not "how many people are self-publishing."**

That's a big deal. It means a lot of these numbers are measuring the wrong thing.

## The "Amazon vs Bowker" chart — what it actually is

Bowker is the US company that sells official ISBNs. The researchers found **two** published numbers from Bowker:

- 2007: 11% of US ISBNs were self-published
- 2012: 40%

That's it. **Two data points.**

They plotted those two dots next to their Amazon data and said "yeah, looks about right."

That's **not** a validation. That's just "the shape looks similar." With two dots you can't check much of anything.

## The tiny-print summary

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Do free ISBNs lead to more self-publishing? | **Maybe.** New Zealand looks like yes, but the comparison is messy and the statistics get weak when you're careful. |
| Did France's 2019 fee change anything? | **Can't tell.** Too short, too noisy, fee too small. |
| Does the official book list show the real self-publishing boom? | **No.** Especially in countries that charge for ISBNs — the boom happens on Amazon and doesn't show up. |
| Is Amazon vs Bowker a real check? | **No.** It's two dots. |

## The one-sentence version

> **Free ISBNs might encourage self-publishing (New Zealand hints at it), but the official book-counting lists mostly miss the Amazon self-publishing boom, so none of the numbers mean quite what they look like — and the France/Québec test didn't really work because France's ISBNs used to be free too.**

## What they should fix before publishing

1. There are some **typos in the tables** (a couple of UK and NZ numbers don't match across pages).
2. The **France/Québec post-2019 table is garbled** in the PDF — it repeats years 2019–2122 like a glitch.
3. The **population numbers are "from memory"** — they literally wrote that in the document. Don't trust the per-person numbers until they use real population data.
4. The fee amounts **don't match** between pages (€637 vs €37).
5. They should stop calling the two-dot Bowker thing "validation."

That's the whole thing, without the statistics.
